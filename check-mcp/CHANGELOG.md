## 0.4.7 — 2026-11-25

**A line now quotes the sentence that carries its word, from the page that holds it, says a second row of the page when there is one, and never says a date before the run in the future tense; and the exported `LEDGER` rows changed shape: `pageWord` holds only the page's own words, or is absent, and what the check wrote in its own words is `ourWord`.** On 0.4.6 `gemini-3.1-flash-image` was called *deprecated* above a table row that does not say so, and on a run after 2026-12-11 `gpt-5` still printed *"is deprecated and shuts down on 2026-12-11"*.

Requires `@warmdrift/kgauto-compiler@2.0.0-alpha.134`, exactly (unchanged).

- **For anyone who reads `LEDGER`:** `pageWord` is optional, and where present it is verbatim on the page the row cites, or on its second page, named in `pageWordUrl`; the ledger generator stops on a row where it is not. On 0.4.6 it held the check's own words on 124 of 326 rows (*"Upcoming deprecations: Deprecated"*, *"listed under Gemini 3 models › Deprecated models"*, a date described in words, a fragment of a link); those strings are now `ourWord`. New fields: `table` (an OpenAI row's table, by its heading), `wordBy` (`date` where the word is the check's reading of a date, `ours` where the page gives no status word), `columns` and `secondRow`. `LEDGER_DEFINITIONS` carries seven more of the providers' sentences.
- **A word from a second page is quoted from that page.** `gemini-3.1-flash-image`, `gemini-3.7-flash`, `gemini-3.5-flash` and `gemini-embedding-2-preview` are *deprecated* by a sentence in Google's release notes, not by their row on the deprecations page. Their lines now quote that sentence, with the page named and the day it was read (*"Google's release notes page (read in this run, …): “Deprecation announcement: The `gemini-3.1-flash-image` model is deprecated (no shutdown date announced).”"*), then the row with its column names, so a release date is not read as a shutdown date (*"Model: … | Release date: May 28, 2026 | Shutdown date: No shutdown date announced | …"*). No other row is quoted by column.
- **A second row is said.** 18 ids sit on two rows of their page (Anthropic's status table and its history, Google's Gemini 3 and Live API tables, OpenAI's past table). The line keeps the first row and adds one sentence quoting the second under its heading. Where the second gives another date or another replacement (`gpt-4-0314`, `code-davinci-002`) the line is *Likely* and says the two rows differ; it never says which is right. A second row is checked on its page like the first.
- **A date before the run is not said in the future.** A row whose date falls after the day its page was read and before the day of the run says the page gives that date and how many days before the run it is (*"is deprecated, and its page gives a shutdown date of 2026-12-11, a date 14 days before this run"*), whether or not the page was read in the run. A date that was already past on the day the page was read keeps its line in the past tense (*"was shut down on 2026-09-28"*): on Google's deprecations page that is Google's own word, since the page says *"Already-shutdown models and agents are listed under the Deprecated models and Deprecated agents sections."*; on OpenAI's table of past deprecations it is the check's reading of a *Shutdown date* that is past, since the page defines *Shutdown* and gives each row the date, with no status word on the row.
- **OpenAI's note on fine-tunes of the legacy GPT models shut down in October 2026 is quoted** for a fine-tune whose base is on the rows the note's own link names: the link ends on the date *2026-04-22*, the notice date of exactly the rows shut down on Oct 23, 2026. A fine-tune of any other past base still says the check quotes no sentence. Four more of OpenAI's sentences about fine-tuned models are read and checked, not printed.
- **An Anthropic id on Amazon Bedrock or Google Cloud quotes Anthropic's own sentences** naming the platforms its dates apply to and those that set their own, then says which side the site's platform falls on: Amazon Bedrock is partner-operated; for Google Cloud the extract does not say whether the Anthropic-managed or the Google-managed offering is used. On 0.4.6 both said the platform runs its own schedule. `--model-facts` says the same of an id written in either form.
- **Whose word, said.** `--model-facts` prints *"<provider>'s word"* only for the page's own word: a word the check read from a date says so (*"this check's word, read from the date on OpenAI's deprecations page: **shut down**"*, for the rows of OpenAI's past table), a row under Google's *Deprecated models* or *Deprecated agents* prints *"Google's word: **shut down**"* with the page's sentence that makes it so, and a date with no status word says *"a shutdown date and no status word on the page"* (0.4.6 printed *"Google's word: **scheduled for shutdown**"*). A line quoting a provider's definition from a page the row does not rest on names that page in its basis.
- **The ledger is re-read** (2026-11-25, 09:06 UTC; 326 rows; no row moved since 2026-11-24). Google's model pages for the four ids its release notes name only in words (`gemini-1.0-pro`, `gemini-pro`, `gemini-1.0-pro-vision`, `gemini-pro-vision`) were requested again the same day (10:01 UTC) and answered 404, as on 2026-11-18; the line says *"Google's model page for this id answered 404 on 2026-11-25."*
- **In the repository, not the package:** `scripts/page-word-sweep.mjs` checks every row's `pageWord` on a saved page set; `scripts/table-sweep.mjs` also counts the second rows said on the card.

## 0.4.4 — 2026-11-18

**Every line a stranger reads is a sentence, a provider's quote is never cut inside a word, and each page is named by its own title.** No kind of finding was added or removed. A provider line now leaves *Certain* in two more cases: an OpenAI model page with more than one id line, and a page whose title is not the one this version expects.

Requires `@warmdrift/kgauto-compiler@2.0.0-alpha.134`, exactly (unchanged).

- **The basis lines are sentences.** Eight of them printed the catalog's own field names (`providerLifecycle`, `maxContextTokens=…`, `parallelToolCalls=false`, `structuredOutput=none`, `retry=none`) or words of ours (*cliff rule*, *portfolio heuristic*). Each now says in words what it rests on: *"your estimate against the model's context limit, 1,048,576 tokens (the catalog's record, checked against Google's documentation on 2026-08-14)"*; *"a threshold the catalog holds for this model (200,000 input tokens; no record of how it was found), against your estimate"*; *"your token estimates; 85% is a rule of thumb from the projects the check's makers run, not a provider's figure"*. Where a threshold came from is read from its record, never from its prose. A test prints every basis the engine can produce and refuses a field name, an `=`, or one of our words.
- **And the other four.** The price line's basis reads *"your token estimates multiplied by the catalog's prices for this model (the catalog's record, checked against OpenAI's documentation on 2026-09-13)"*, and its detail gives the prices' source in the same words (it said *doc-verified prices … verified <date>*). The cheaper-models line: *"your token estimates at each model's prices in the catalog; nothing on this line says how well any of them would do your work"*. The observe-day line: *"this check reads the extract of your code and runs none of your calls, so it cannot see how they behave"*. An id not recognised: *"this id was looked up in the catalog and in the providers' pages this check reads, and matched neither"*.
- **No *cliff* and no *probe* on a card's line.** The threshold line's title reads *"`gpt-6-astra`: the catalog holds a threshold at 272,000 input tokens, and this site crosses it"* (it said *"has a known input tokens cliff at 272,000"*), and its detail says *a test* where it said *a probe*, in the words the basis uses (*"Evidence: a test reproduced the problem, last run 2026-11-12"*; *"… holds that back until a test reproduces the problem"*). A catalog reason the line quotes is the compiler's text and is quoted as it is.
- **The header says its counting rule when the rule did something.** When a flagged id is written in more than one kind of place, one sentence after the counts says so: *"1 of them is written in more than one kind of place, and is counted once, under the first that applies in this order: code that runs, where the extract does not say, tests, examples, docs."* Otherwise no sentence is added.
- **A quote is the page's words, or it is marked.** The card cut a provider's line at 240 characters, inside a word: Google's reroute of `gemini-2.5-flash` ended *"…will automatically route to `gemi"*. A quote is now printed whole up to 300 characters (every row of this ledger is shorter), and a longer one is cut at its last sentence end or table-cell boundary, outside any code mark, and ends with *…*. `--model-facts` prints the quote the same way.
- **The header says what each number counts.** *"0 AI call sites and 8 model ids: …. 7 are flagged by their providers, and 1 by this check's reading of Google's release notes page. Of the 8 flagged ids, 3 are in code that runs, 1 in tests, 1 in docs, 1 in examples and 2 where the extract does not say."* One provider is named (*"1 is flagged by OpenAI."*). No *(s)* anywhere on a card.
- **The line names the page that was not read.** When a row rests on two pages and only one was read in this run, the line and its basis say which: *"In this run OpenAI's deprecations page was read (…) and still says this, and OpenAI's model page for GPT-4o mini was not read (not the page this version reads)"*. In 0.4.3 the line said the whole row was not read in this run. Such a line stays *Likely*.
- **A model page with two id lines is not the page.** An OpenAI model page is read only when it carries exactly one `Model ID:` line, naming the model its address names; 0.4.3 judged a page with two by the first.
- **Each page is called what its own title calls it.** The ledger's source list carries each page's title, read from the page by the generator and asserted as part of its shape (a page whose title changes is not the page this version reads): Anthropic's *Model deprecations*, OpenAI's *Deprecations* and four model pages, Google's *Gemini deprecations* and *Release notes*, DeepSeek's *Change Log* and *Your First API Call*. The card says *"Google's release notes page"* where it said *"Google's changelog"*, and *"DeepSeek's “Your First API Call” page"* for the address `/news`, which still carries the sentence two rows quote and `/updates` does not.
- **The ledger is re-read** (2026-11-18, 18:23 UTC): five of the ten pages differ in bytes from the 2026-11-17 read (Anthropic's, Google's two, DeepSeek's two), and no row, definition or page title moved (307 rows).
- **In the repository, not the package:** `scripts/smoke-share.mjs` posts to the production endpoint only with `--production`, and otherwise needs `KGAUTO_SHARE_ENDPOINT_OVERRIDE` or exits 1 having sent nothing; the three smokes remove their temporary folders; every sentence the ledger generator types is checked on its saved page before it writes; DeepSeek's rows keep the page's own words for their status (*"legacy API model names"*, *"have been retired"*), checked inside their sentence and on the saved page, where the legacy rows held a paraphrase.

## 0.4.3 — 2026-11-16

**The check now makes network requests by default: each run reads the same ten public provider pages (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, DeepSeek), whatever your project holds; `--offline` (or `KGAUTO_CHECK_OFFLINE=1`, or `offline: true` on the server's tools) stops it.** Nothing about your project is sent; a provider line is *Certain* only when its page was read in this run and still carries it.

Requires `@warmdrift/kgauto-compiler@2.0.0-alpha.134`, exactly.

- **Certain only on a page read in this run.** The check reads the ledger's ten source pages at run time (plain GET, redirects followed, no cookie, no query string, a User-Agent of `kgauto-check/<version>`, all at once, 8 s a page, no retry, nothing cached or written). A ledger line is *Certain* only when every quote its row rests on is on its page in this run and the site is on the provider's own API. A page that did not answer, timed out, or answered with a page of another shape (a consent page, a login page, a rewrite) leaves its lines *Likely*, saying the day this version read the page and why it was not read now; a page that no longer carries a line marks it *as read on <day> (the page has changed since)* and says to read the page before acting on the date. A catalog line (the model page's word, a limit, a capability) is *Likely* in every run: its page is not read at run time, and the line carries the day it was. The footer says how many of the pages were read; the stamp says when.
- **Where an id is written.** The extract may say, per call site and per listed id, where it is written: `runs`, `test`, `docs` or `example`. The header counts the flagged ids by it; each site prints it in brackets; findings in code that runs come first; and a finding only in tests, docs or examples prints the ℹ️ mark. An extract that says nothing about it gets one sentence saying the count is of every place an id appears.
- **`share_card`**, a fifth tool on the server: what `--share` does on the CLI, for an MCP run — offered after the card, called only on your human's yes (`humanSaidYes: true`). No tool was removed or renamed.
- **A changelog row.** `gemini-pro`, `gemini-1.0-pro`, `gemini-pro-vision` and `gemini-1.0-pro-vision`, which 0.4.2 called *not recognised*, now print Google's changelog's sentence (*"Gemini 1.0 Pro is no longer supported."*, February 18, 2025; *"Support for Gemini 1.0 Pro Vision removed from Google AI services and tools."*, July 12, 2024), always *Likely*: the changelog names the model in words, and the link from the id to the name is ours. The line says Google's model page for the id answered 404 when this version was built.
- **The ledger is re-read** (2026-11-16, 22:54 UTC): no row moved; 303 rows, and the four changelog rows above (307).
- **The page's shape is asserted** by the ledger generator and by the run-time read, with one function: the table columns, the headings and the status words each page's rows are read from.
- **The words.** The package no longer calls itself offline. What it says of itself: *it reads your repository on your machine; nothing about your project leaves it unless you choose to share the result.* The stored-row sentence reads *"Sharing stores one row: …"*, the same on the CLI and the server.

## 0.4.1 — 2026-11-14

**A cliff line says what its evidence says, and the catalog no longer moves under the check.** From a cold read of 0.4.0 on 2026-11-14 by a reader that built none of it (the read is kept in the private working tree).

Requires `@warmdrift/kgauto-compiler@2.0.0-alpha.133`, exactly.

- **A cliff line is worded from the cliff.** It used to say *"quality degrades in a measured, repeatable way"* for every catalog cliff — including a request the provider refuses (`gpt-6-luna` with tools, HTTP 400 on one probe) and two price-tier rules. Now it quotes the rule's own reason, says what the compiler does about it in plain words (and when the compiler holds that back until a probe confirms the cliff), says the evidence (probed and reproduced, observed, carried over, or no record) with its date and count where the catalog gives them, and names the endpoint when the rule is about one. The quality sentence stays only for a quality rule on observed or probe-confirmed evidence. A rule about structured output speaks only where the site parses structured output, as the compiler applies it. A cliff line is never *certain*.
- **The catalog is pinned.** The compiler dependency is an exact version, so the card a stranger gets from `npx` is the card the tests saw. A regression set of 40 extracts from public projects compares each card with a stored one, so the next catalog bump is a diff someone reads. It is kept in the private working tree, outside this package, until the maintainers of those projects have heard from us; the package's own tests cover each kind of card line on made-up extracts.
- **A gateway is not the provider's own API.** An id written as `openrouter/…` gets its lifecycle line at *likely*, with the sentence that OpenRouter may keep its own schedule for the model and its page. A bare `provider/` prefix (`anthropic/`, `openai/` …) is unchanged.
- **The printed commands name the version.** The usage text and the extract contract print `npx -y @warmdrift/kgauto-check@0.4.1 …`.
- **Two sentences.** A model already shut down reads *"has been shut down (the earliest shutdown date the page gave was …)"*, without *not before*. The *Likely* heading names all three kinds of line under it.
- **MIT.** The licence is MIT, and `LICENSE` ships in the package.
- **`--share` says what it stores**, where it is offered and where it is confirmed: a row number, the time it arrived, the project name, the extract, the card, the check's version and the catalog's version. It counts as sent only when the endpoint answers `{ok: true, id}`. `scripts/smoke-share.mjs` performs that write once, for a made-up project; it is not part of `npm test`.
- **No internal application's name on a card.** A cliff rule whose catalog note names one of our own applications is not quoted: the line says the note is written for our own records, and still gives the action and the evidence.
- **After the cold read (Amendment B).** A cliff line says the site is *at the line* when its figure equals the threshold, *over* it only when greater. The "quality degrades in a measured, repeatable way" sentence needs a recorded observation or a confirming probe, not a word in the rule's prose (on the pinned catalog no rule carries it). A card run with `--share` no longer says nothing was uploaded: its footer says the card and the extract are sent after it is printed; a share the endpoint does not confirm says a row may or may not exist. On a gateway or a partner platform, a limit, a capability or a price read from the provider's page is *likely* and says whose figure it is. The README's commands name the version.

The fix at the source — the reason text in the compiler's catalog, now reader-facing, including its internal nouns (*"carried from the brain row"* on `gpt-5.6-luna`) — is carried to the next compiler filing.
